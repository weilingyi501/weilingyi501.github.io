Fiscal Policy

Government spending and taxes affect demand, sometimes automatically and sometimes by choice. The debate concerns whether they should be used to stabilize the economy.

In this topic
  1. Budgets and demand
  2. Automatic and discretionary
  3. The case against active policy
  4. The case for it
  5. Military spending
  6. Is debt a burden?
  7. Readings

1. Budgets and demand

Fiscal policy is government spending and taxation. A deficit puts more into the spending stream than taxes take out, and a surplus does the reverse. The debt is the sum of past deficits less surpluses. Transfers such as Social Security and interest move income without buying goods.

2. Automatic and discretionary

Automatic policy works under existing law. In a recession unemployment benefits rise and tax receipts fall, so the deficit widens without a vote; in an expansion it shrinks. The text’s averages for 1970–2001 show this in peacetime. War is the great exception, since spending jumps and taxes lag. Discretionary policy needs new decisions.

3. The case against active policy

Milton Friedman argued that the economy corrects itself, that public spending takes resources that would have been used privately (crowding out), and that the government’s proper tasks are limited. Delays are a further problem: recognizing a downturn, passing a law, and building a bridge may take longer than the recession.

4. The case for it

Keynesians answer that idle resources are not crowded out and that waiting is costly. The simple rule is to spend more or tax less in a slump and to do the opposite in a boom. The text adds limits of its own: private interests resist many kinds of public spending, tax cuts for high incomes are spent slowly, and one policy cannot fight unemployment and inflation at once.

5. Military spending

War production ended the Depression. The authors argue that military spending meets less opposition than civilian spending and so has often filled the gap in demand, but that at full employment it displaces consumption and investment. They favor replacing it with civilian spending; others see it as set mainly by security needs.

6. Is debt a burden?

A government that borrows in its own currency cannot be forced into default, but it can cause inflation. Interest moves income from taxpayers to bondholders. Debt owed abroad is a claim on future output in a way that debt owed at home is not.

Note: Large fiscal programs in 2009 and 2020–21 have produced much new evidence. Many studies find larger multipliers when interest rates are near zero, and economists disagree about how much the 2021 package added to inflation.

Question to think about: Should the government balance its budget every year?

7. Readings

Companion reading
