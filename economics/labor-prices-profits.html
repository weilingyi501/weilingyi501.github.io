Labor, Prices, and Profits

Smith and Ricardo linked prices to labor, and Marx used the idea to explain profit. The chapters set out that theory and the objections to it, including those of sympathetic economists.

  2. What sets wages
  3. Labor and price

1. Necessary and surplus labor

In the classical and Marxian account the working day has two parts. In necessary labor time employees produce the equivalent of their pay, and in surplus labor time they produce the employer’s profit. The ratio of the two is called the rate of exploitation, a technical term here that can apply to high-paid as well as low-paid work. The text applies the same division to slavery and serfdom to show how the form of the surplus differs across systems.

2. What sets wages

Pay lies between a ceiling, the full product, and a floor set by subsistence and custom. Within that range the text stresses bargaining over hours, pay, and the intensity of work. Bargaining power depends on unemployment, unions, the safety net, law, and public opinion. Union membership in the United States fell from over a third of employees in the mid-1950s to about 13 percent in the early 2000s.

3. Labor and price

Smith imagined a simple society where goods exchange according to the labor they take. If a pig takes twice the work of a chicken, one pig trades for two chickens. Under capitalism prices also cover materials and profit. The labor theory holds that competitive prices in the long run reflect total labor time, past and present, with profit coming from the surplus part.

4. The transformation problem

If every industry has the same rate of surplus but uses different amounts of materials per worker, labor values imply different profit rates. Yet competition tends to equalize profit rates. Prices must therefore depart from labor values, and how to connect the two has been debated for more than a century.

5. Alternatives

Some economists who accept that a surplus exists reject the labor theory of price. Sraffa, following Ricardo, derived prices from the physical conditions of production and distribution. Roemer used neoclassical tools to define exploitation through unequal ownership. Most neoclassical economists reject the concept: for them, a wage in a competitive market already equals the worker’s contribution (see topic 21).

Question to think about: Can a theory of profit stand without the labor theory of value?

6. Readings
