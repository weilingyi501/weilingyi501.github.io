Capitalism: Institutions and Ideologies

How capitalism came into being and changed, and how each stage produced its own defenders and critics. A course built around E. K. Hunt’s Property and Prophets: The Evolution of Economic Institutions and Ideologies.

About the course

E. K. Hunt is an emeritus professor of economics at the University of Utah, and his textbooks have introduced many students to the subject. His History of Economic Thought follows theories. Property and Prophets is different: it tells the economic history of capitalism and the history of ideas about it together, chapter by chapter, so that each set of ideas appears in the conditions that produced it. Hunt does not argue that history mechanically determines ideas. He places the two side by side and asks readers to draw their own conclusions.

This course follows the book’s fourteen chapters, one topic to each, and adds a fifteenth on the years since its last edition in 2003. Every topic explains Hunt’s account in summary and then goes beyond it, setting out later scholarship, the points on which historians and economists disagree with him, and a few factual slips in the text. The pages are meant to be read with the book and do not replace it. For the theories themselves, see the companion course on the history of economic thought.

Three questions run through the whole course:

InstitutionsWho owns the means of production, who works, and who receives the surplus?
IdeologiesWhich ideas make this arrangement seem natural or just, and where did they come from?
CriticsWho opposed it, in the name of which values, and with what result?

Learning goals

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  1. Describe the main stages in the development of capitalism, from the decline of feudalism to the present.
  2. Explain the major ideologies that defended each stage, and trace how older ideas return in new forms.
  3. Explain the major criticisms of capitalism and the disagreements among its critics.
  4. Evaluate Hunt’s arguments against later evidence and against the strongest case on the other side.
  5. Use the same method to analyze a present-day debate.

Readings

Main text

E. K. Hunt, Property and Prophets: The Evolution of Economic Institutions and Ideologies, updated 7th ed., with a foreword by Robert Pollin (M. E. Sharpe, 2003).

Companion texts

E. K. Hunt and Mark Lautzenheiser, History of Economic Thought: A Critical Perspective, 3rd ed. (2011).

Robert Heilbroner, The Worldly Philosophers, 7th ed. (1999).

Karl Polanyi, The Great Transformation (1944).

Joyce Appleby, The Relentless Revolution: A History of Capitalism (2010).

Thomas Piketty, Capital and Ideology (2019).

Primary sources

Short selections from Aquinas, Mandeville, Smith, Malthus, Owen, Marx, Carnegie, Veblen, Bernstein, Luxemburg, Lenin, Keynes, Friedman, and Baran and Sweezy.
